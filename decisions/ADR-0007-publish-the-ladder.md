# ADR-0007: Publish the full commission ladder

Date: 31 July 2026. Status: accepted. **Supersedes ADR-0006.**

## Context

ADR-0006 concluded that one price should be published and the Stack A/B/C ladder
kept internal, on the strength of master plan v4 section 6.3 and directive 8 of
`reference/plan-v4-directives.md` ("kill tier names + price grids from
client-facing framing").

The founder has overruled that conclusion directly:

> Publish the pricing. Overrule the plan. It was generated by Claude and I don't
> agree with it.

That is decisive, and it is worth being precise about why. The plan is not a
source of external authority. It was drafted in this repository, its pricing rule
was a judgement call rather than a finding, and the person carrying the commercial
risk disagrees with the judgement. There is no evidence on either side yet — no
letters of intent, no enquiry data, no competitor response — so the argument in
ADR-0006 was a hypothesis presented with more confidence than it had earned.

## Decision

Publish the full ladder, client-facing, at `/commission/`.

| Published | |
|---|---|
| Foundation | A$975,000, ex-donor |
| Grande Traversée | A$1,150,000, ex-donor |
| Signature | From A$1,600,000, ex-donor |
| Founders series | First three at A$895,000, at Foundation specification |
| All in, with a donor sourced by us | A$1.07m to A$1.11m |

The four option lines the plan withheld are published with them: left-hand-drive
conversion A$85,000–120,000, GCC thermal package A$45,000–60,000, export
compliance and logistics A$25,000–40,000, ownership programme A$18,000 a year.

The payment schedule is published in full: A$100,000 on signing, then 30% / 25%
/ 25% against the shell, painted-body and trimmed gates, balance on
certification and delivery.

Plan v4 section 5.6 rule 4 ("no pricing is ever published") is void. Directive 8
is void as to pricing. Nothing else in either document is affected.

## Rationale

Two things in the ADR-0006 argument do not survive contact with the counter-case.

**"A grid invites shopping" assumes the comparison is avoidable.** It is not. A
buyer at A$1m is going to look at Theon, Wiedergeboren and Zeigler whether or not
this site has a price on it. Withholding the number does not prevent the
comparison; it just means the buyer runs it without our figures in it, using
whatever a forum told them. Publishing puts the frame in our hands, and the frame
is where the position is actually won: Theon does not publish a 1,500 km
validation protocol or a certification file bound into the book.

**"A figure filters enquiries" argues for publishing more, not less.** ADR-0006
accepted that a price filters, then published the smallest possible amount of
price. If filtering is the goal, the ladder filters better than a single "from"
figure, because a "from" figure tells a buyer nothing about where they land and
invites the one email that a price page exists to prevent.

Against those, the honest cost of publishing: the three prices are a public
commitment made before car one exists, and both A$975,000 and A$1,150,000 remain
contingent on ADR-0003 (labour rate) and ADR-0004 (LCT). That risk is real and it
is not resolved by this decision. It is stated below rather than argued away.

## Consequences

`/commission/` is now the site's single conversion destination. The previous build
sent every call to action to a `mailto:` link, which needs a configured mail
client, captures nothing and confirms nothing to the sender. The enquiry form
lives on this page, below the prices, so an enquiry arrives from someone who has
already seen the number.

**The published figures are now a repricing exposure.** An adverse LCT ruling
implies roughly A$1.3m for the Foundation commission, and a labour rate at the
top of the market moves it further. Once these numbers are public, a revision is
visible and has to be explained, where before it was a private conversation. The
mitigation is sequencing, not wording: ADR-0004's private ruling should be
obtained before the site is put on a domain, so the first published number is the
post-ruling one.

The internal Stack A/B/C economics are unchanged, and the model is unchanged.
What has changed is who can see them.

**Signature is published as "From A$1,600,000" rather than a fixed figure**,
because it is genuinely bespoke and a fixed number there would be the one
published price we could not stand behind. The figure is plan v4 section 6.3's
Stack C guide price ("A$1.6m+") unchanged.

**Publishing Signature surfaces a collision the internal ladder was hiding.**
A$1.6m is Zeigler/Bailey's price to the dollar (section 3.2, table row 4). While
the ladder was internal that was a costing coincidence; published, it is a
side-by-side against an Australian house that has already sold eight cars, with a
billet 4.4L engine, on the same G-body, at the same number. The pricing wedge this
venture is built on exists at Foundation and evaporates at Signature. Either
Signature needs a differentiator that reads at A$1.6m without a track record, or
it should be the tier that stays "quoted privately". Flagging it as an open
question rather than settling it here, because it does not block the Foundation
commission, which is the only tier car one can be.

If this is reversed, the revert is contained: `web/pages_commission.py` holds
every figure as module-level data, and the summary block in `web/index.html` is
the only other place a price appears.
