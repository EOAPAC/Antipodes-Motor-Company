## 3. Competitive price ladder, reconciled to a common tax basis

On a like-for-like ex-GST basis Antipode undercuts Wiedergeboren by A$175,000, or 15.2% below it, rather than the ~A$290k implied by comparing against the GST-inclusive figure. That is still a real wedge, but it is thinner than PLAN-A assumes, and it is the wedge that an adverse LCT ruling erases: at A$1,370,080 all-in, Antipode would sit 8.3% above Wiedergeboren instead of below it.

Theon sits below both Australian houses on price while delivering a full carbon body over 6,000 hours. Any Australian house pricing above Theon is selling locality, certification and access to the builder, not a cost advantage. That is a defensible pitch, and it is worth being clear internally that it is the actual pitch.

## 6. Luxury Car Tax exposure

Rate and threshold are legislated (PLAN-B: 33% above A$80,567 GST-inclusive, 2025-26 general threshold). Whether a re-manufactured classic is in scope is unresolved in both plans and is the question the ATO private ruling must answer. The arithmetic below shows why it cannot be left open.

| Measure | LCT out of scope | LCT in scope |
|---|---|---|
| Commission price, ex-GST | A$975,000 | A$975,000 |
| GST | A$97,500 | A$97,500 |
| LCT | A$0 | A$297,580 |
| **Buyer pays, ex-donor** | **A$1,072,500** | **A$1,370,080** |
| Gross margin to Antipode | 34.9% | 34.9% |

Passing LCT through costs Antipode no margin, but it lifts the buyer's ex-donor cost to A$1,370,080, above Wiedergeboren's A$1.265m. The entire pricing strategy is to sit below Wiedergeboren, so passing the tax on inverts the position.

Absorbing it instead, to hold the buyer at A$1,072,500, drops the ex-GST commission to A$766,902 and the margin from 34.9% to 17.2%:

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Ex-GST commission price | A$766,902 |
| Total COGS (scenario A) | A$635,000 |
| Gross profit per car | A$131,902 |
| Gross margin | 17.2% |
| Break-even cars against lean fixed | 3.4 |

So an adverse ruling either breaks the price position or roughly halves the margin. There is no version where it is a minor variable, and it is resolvable for the cost of a private ruling before the first invoice.

## 10. The three-tier commission ladder

A separate concept (see `docs/08-concept-review.md`) proposed three price points instead of one, on the reasoning that a lower entry tier widens a thin buyer pool. Tested here, because it is the one genuinely new commercial idea in that concept and it changes the answer to risk R9.

Each tier carries its own specification, so COGS moves with price. Hours and the non-labour delta are assumptions and are the first thing to replace with real quotes.

### At the internal cost rate (A$120/hr)

| Tier | Price | Hours | COGS | Gross profit | Margin | Specification |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Heritage | A$850,000 | 2,400 | A$533,000 | A$317,000 | 37.3% | Rebuilt 3.4L, standard arches, trim to sample. No widebody, no carbon |
| Signature | A$1,150,000 | 3,000 | A$635,000 | A$515,000 | 44.8% | The specified car: subtle widebody, full upgrade menu |
| Export | A$1,600,000 | 3,800 | A$826,000 | A$774,000 | 48.4% | Adds LHD conversion, homologation and freight. Out of Phase 0 scope |

### At a market shop rate (A$160/hr)

| Tier | Price | Hours | COGS | Gross profit | Margin | Specification |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Heritage | A$850,000 | 2,400 | A$629,000 | A$221,000 | 26.0% | Rebuilt 3.4L, standard arches, trim to sample. No widebody, no carbon |
| Signature | A$1,150,000 | 3,000 | A$755,000 | A$395,000 | 34.3% | The specified car: subtle widebody, full upgrade menu |
| Export | A$1,600,000 | 3,800 | A$978,000 | A$622,000 | 38.9% | Adds LHD conversion, homologation and freight. Out of Phase 0 scope |

The entry tier carries its weight on margin and still fails on positioning. At A$850,000 the Heritage car returns 37.3% at internal cost, which clears the ~35% constraint, because the lighter specification takes 600 fewer hours. So the objection is not the margin at cost. It is two other things.

First, fragility: at a market labour rate the same car falls to 26.0%, the worst of the three tiers, so the entry tier is where the unresolved labour-rate question (R1) bites hardest. Second, and more serious, A$850,000 lands within A$10,000 of Theon Design, which delivers a full carbon-bodied car over roughly 6,000 hours for about A$860,000 ex-GST. Pricing there invites a direct comparison against a more deeply specified car, and it is a comparison we lose on paper.

The Signature tier is the strong one: A$515,000 gross at 44.8%, well above the single-price A$975,000 plan, because price rises faster than hours do. Together those two findings point somewhere specific, and away from both source plans as written: two tiers, not three and not one.

| Recommended ladder | Price | Hours | COGS | Gross profit | Margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Foundation | A$975,000 | 3,000 | A$635,000 | A$340,000 | 34.9% |
| Signature | A$1,150,000 | 3,400 | A$723,000 | A$427,000 | 37.1% |

Both sit at or just above the ~35% constraint, the A$175,000 gap between them is wide enough to mean something to a buyer without inviting a Theon comparison, and the Foundation price is the one already benchmarked against Wiedergeboren. Drop the A$850,000 entry tier, and defer Export until a second market is genuinely in scope.
