# Business Plan: An Ultra-Luxury Porsche 911 Restomod Atelier for Australia

Prepared for Jeremy Levitt and Luigi Pacelli, co-founders and prospective co-investors. Australian English throughout. July 2026.

## TL;DR

- **Build it, and call it Antipode.** We recommend launching an Australian ultra-luxury Porsche 911 restomod house named **Antipode** (traded as “Antipode Motor Co.”), building bespoke restomods on plentiful, galvanised 1979 to 1985 G-body donors (911 SC and early 3.2 Carrera) out of Luigi Pacelli’s Artarmon workshop, at a launch commission price of **A$975,000 plus a donor car**, deliberately positioned between Theon Design (about A$860,000 equivalent, ex-donor) and the local Wiedergeboren (A$1.265m, ex-donor).
- **The edge is real and hard to copy.** The founders already own an accredited master-technician workshop (removing the biggest capital and hiring barrier), a guaranteed-editorial PR engine (Baden Bower), an awards-credibility platform (Global Recognition Awards) and cheap high-quality offshore labour (BruntWork). No local rival combines a Sydney master-tech workshop with a global demand-generation machine.
- **The gaps are equally real.** Pacelli’s public business, European Galleria, is a mechanical service and engine-rebuild workshop, not a paint, panel, carbon and trim atelier, and there is no engineering signatory yet. Those capabilities, plus a NSW VSCCS certifier, an ATO ruling on Luxury Car Tax, and trademark clearance for the name, must be secured before the first car is sold.

## Key Findings

1. **The donor thesis is validated by the market.** G-body 3.2 Carreras (about 74,000 built) and 911 SCs (about 60,000 built) are the most plentiful and affordable air-cooled 911 chassis in Australia. Even the local incumbent, Wiedergeboren, concedes “964s are hard to find; there’s a lot more chassis in the 3.2s.”  Local G-body donors average near A$114,000 versus roughly A$175,875 for a 964. The G-body’s galvanised steel unibody is the single most important durability advantage for a car meant to be driven hard in a hot, coarse-road climate.
1. **The buyer pool is small but sufficient, and export widens it.** LGT Wealth Management’s 2025 State of Wealth report (produced with Investment Trends, surveying 1,191 high-net-worth Australians including 168 ultra-high-net-worth) found the high-net-worth population grew 10% to 760,000 people in the year to June 2025, with total investable assets up 18% to A$4 trillion. Within that, 34,500 Australians are ultra-high-net-worth (more than A$10m), a cohort that grew 19% in the year. A domestic buyer pool in the low tens of cars per year is defensible before any export, and the United States 25-year import exemption lets a 1979 to 1985 car be sold freely into the world’s deepest restomod market.
1. **The partnership’s assets are complementary and genuinely scarce.** Luigi Pacelli brings a factory-trained (Ferrari and Maserati) master-tech workshop operating since 2012, with engine-rebuild and mechanical capability. Jeremy Levitt brings Baden Bower (guaranteed named placements in Forbes, Robb Report, Vanity Fair and the like), BruntWork (offshore CAD, content, CRM and lead handling at low hourly cost) and Global Recognition Awards (third-party credibility). Together they solve the hardest problem in seven-figure car sales, which is credible reach to buyers.
1. **The honest gaps are paint/panel, trim, carbon, design authority and an engineering signatory.** European Galleria markets mechanical service and engine work, not coachbuilding. These must be brought in-house or contracted under tight quality control before build one.
1. **Regulation and IP are manageable but must be handled deliberately.** Retain and repair the original galvanised shell and VIN (never replace the tub) to avoid Individually Constructed Vehicle reclassification; engage a NSW VSCCS-licensed certifier on retainer from day one; obtain an ATO private ruling on Luxury Car Tax before the first sale; and always market “a Porsche 911 reimagined by Antipode” with non-affiliation disclaimers.

### Product definition: the car

Focus the donor on 1979 to 1985 to stay in the classic-silhouette, pre-Getrag 915-gearbox sweet spot with the widest supply. The build resolves the G-body’s known weaknesses and turns them into story: the pre-1984 non-pressure-fed timing-chain tensioner (replaced with the factory’s own pressure-fed Carrera-style tensioner and oil feed), the CIS airbox backfire risk (pop-off valve), the rubber-centred clutch (replace with sprung), Dilavar head-stud fatigue on high-mileage engines, notchy 915 synchros, and ageing oil lines.

Specification philosophy: period-correct silhouette; subtle wide body; a rebuilt and upgraded air-cooled flat-six with a drivable output in the 210 to 260 kW range prioritising mid-range torque (in the spirit of Wiedergeboren’s road-focused tune rather than a headline peak); five-speed manual; uprated brakes and suspension; 17 to 18-inch Fuchs-style forged wheels; a cabin of leather and exposed metal with modern audio hidden. The signature is restraint, not aggression. Do not chase a billet 4.4L arms race with Z/B; win on taste, provenance, drivability and story.

### Operations and build process at Artarmon

The build runs out of European Galleria’s Artarmon workshop as the mechanical, assembly and engine-build hub. Flow: donor acquisition and inspection; full teardown and bare-metal strip; body and paint (contracted specialist under Antipode quality control, or a hired panel/paint lead); mechanical and engine rebuild with the upgrade menu (in-house, the workshop’s core strength); trim and interior (specialist sub-contract); final assembly, calibration and road development (in-house); VSCCS certification and registration; delivery or export.

Capacity discipline is the operational crux. The existing service business is the base-load revenue and must not be starved by restomod builds, and equally the builds must not be crowded out by servicing. Ring-fence build bays, technician hours and a separate scheduling board for Antipode work, and treat the two businesses as separate P&Ls sharing a roof. Two cars in year one is deliberately modest so the service business, cash flow and quality systems are not stressed while the process is proven. BruntWork staff handle CAD and 3D configurator modelling, the build-journal content pipeline, CRM, first-line enquiry handling and asset production, freeing the Sydney team for build and client work.

### Regulatory and IP plan for NSW

Significantly modified vehicles in NSW must be certified under the Vehicle Safety Compliance Certification Scheme (VSCCS) by a Transport for NSW-licensed certifier, against VSB14/NCOP and the modification codes in Vehicle Standards Information sheet 6. Engine swaps, brake and suspension upgrades, body modifications and seat/belt changes all fall within licensed modification codes, and certifiers are only authorised within their licensed sub-categories. Engage a certifier whose licence covers the full set of modifications, on retainer, involved from build one so staged inspection photographs and evidence are captured as work proceeds.

ICV risk: if the original tub is replaced rather than repaired, the car risks Individually Constructed Vehicle treatment, a heavier and more expensive path. The operational rule is absolute: retain, repair and seam-strengthen the original galvanised G-body shell and its VIN. This also protects identity, resale integrity and the “restored, not built-from-scratch” story.

Luxury Car Tax: LCT generally does not apply to vehicles more than 25 years old, but there is genuine risk that a newly re-manufactured vehicle is treated differently. Do not guess. Obtain an ATO private binding ruling on the LCT treatment of a G-body restomod before the first sale, and price with a contingency until the ruling is in hand. (This round’s search budget did not allow independent re-verification of the current ATO position; flagged as an open item.)

Import rules: the Australian 25-year rule covers all donors in the window; the US 25-year exemption covers export of the finished pre-2001 cars to America.

Trademark exposure: never market or invoice “a Porsche.” Always “a Porsche 911 reimagined by Antipode,” with the non-affiliation disclaimer on every page, document and social asset. The Singer matter is instructive: Porsche Cars North America filed suit against Singer Vehicle Design (No. 1:24-cv-00253, US District Court for the District of Delaware, filed 26 February 2024) over 911 trade dress; Porsche withdrew the suit on 22 March 2024 after Singer amended its website wording (adding language that builds are done “at the direction of its clients”). The lesson is precise: keep the Antipode identity dominant, avoid Porsche crests and script, avoid copying protected design cues wholesale, frame everything as client-directed restoration, and clear the brand name in class 12 and 37 before any public launch.

1. A **separate arm’s-length services agreement** so European Galleria is paid a market labour rate for build hours and the service business is compensated for bays and staff. This prevents Luigi being effectively diluted twice (equity plus donated labour) and keeps the two P&Ls clean.
1. Baden Bower, BruntWork and Global Recognition Awards provide services to the JV at cost or as documented in-kind contributions, credited to Jeremy’s capital account, so the value is transparent.
1. Founder vesting (for example four years) and standard leaver provisions, given the acute key-person dependence on Luigi.

### Go-to-market plan

**Phase 1, credibility and asset creation (months 0 to 3).** Build the brand, homepage and AI render suite (BruntWork CAD and content). Secure the name and disclaimers. Commission a Global Recognition Award to seed third-party credibility before any product exists.

**Phase 2, launch PR (months 3 to 6).** Weaponise Baden Bower’s guaranteed placements: a coordinated wave in Forbes, Robb Report and the business and luxury press timed to the first render reveal and, ideally, the first physical car. The message is “Australia finally has a world-class 911 restomod house, and here is why the G-body is the smart choice.” Every placement carries the non-affiliation line.

**Phase 3, collector-network activation (ongoing).** Direct presence where the buyers already are: Porsche Club NSW events, concours and track days, and a curated presence on Collecting Cars and Shannons auctions (both to source donors and to be seen by the buyer pool). The Sydney North Shore location, near Autohaus Hamilton in Chatswood (Sydney’s longest-established independent Porsche specialist, since 1970), sits Antipode inside the densest concentration of Porsche wealth and enthusiasts in the country. Autohaus is a servicing and sales competitor for attention, not a restomod builder, and can be a donor-sourcing and referral relationship rather than a rival.

**Phase 4, export (year 2 onward).** Use the US 25-year exemption and Baden Bower’s US placement reach to open American commissions once domestic proof points exist.

## Recommendations

1. **Proceed, but stage the commitment.** The economics work at two cars a year because the workshop and its fixed costs already exist. Commit capital to brand, render assets and the first donor now; do not commit to a five- or six-car ramp until build one is delivered and certified and at least one paid foundation commission is banked.
1. **Adopt Antipode, subject to clearance, with Verrada as the pre-agreed fallback.** Run the class 12/37, ASIC and WHOIS checks in the first 30 days so branding is not built on an unverified name.
1. **Close the capability gaps before build one.** Contract paint/body and trim specialists under Antipode quality control, hire or contract a design authority, and put a VSCCS certifier on retainer. These are the difference between a world-class car and a liability.
1. **Structure the JV as a clean 50/50 entity with a tie-break and a separate services agreement**, so Luigi is paid market labour rates on top of equity and neither founder is diluted unfairly.
1. **De-risk tax and IP up front.** Lodge the ATO private ruling on LCT and secure the trademark clearance and disclaimer regime before the first invoice, not after.
1. **Price at A$975,000 for the foundation series, then step toward A$1.15m** as the order book fills, holding the ~35% gross margin as the governing constraint.

## Caveats

Several figures are planning assumptions, clearly labelled, and must be replaced with real quotes: build hours, internal labour rate, outsourced paint/trim costs, and the five-year volumes. European Galleria’s actual service revenue was not available and should anchor the base-load numbers. Trademark and domain availability for Antipode (and all candidates) were not verified in this project and require the manual IP Australia, ASIC and WHOIS checks specified. The current Luxury Car Tax treatment of a re-manufactured classic could not be independently re-verified this round and needs an ATO private ruling. Review counts and some biographical claims about Luigi Pacelli and European Galleria come partly from syndicated PR-style articles with inconsistent numbers (25 vs 50 vs 54 reviews; 4.9 vs 5.0 rating) and should be confirmed against primary sources and live Google listings. Critically, the “European Automotive” name used in the brief does not match a distinct verifiable trading entity in Artarmon; the publicly identifiable business run by Luigi Pacelli is **European Galleria** (Level 1, 40 Dickson Avenue, Artarmon, founded 2012), and the ABN could not be confirmed.
