## 2. Executive Summary / TL;DR

- **Build it, and call it Antipode.** An Australian ultra-luxury Porsche 911 restomod house, traded as **Antipode Motor Co.**, building one-of-one commissions on plentiful, galvanised 1979–85 G-body donors (911 SC and early 3.2 Carrera) out of Luigi Pacelli's Artarmon workshop. Launch commission price **A$975,000 ex-donor** (client all-in ~A$1.07–1.11m against a donor at A$90–130k), positioned between Theon Design (~A$860k ex-donor) and the local Wiedergeboren (A$1.265m ex-donor), well under Z/B (A$1.6m) and Singer (from ~US$1.5m).
- **The edge is real and hard to copy.** The founders already own an accredited master-technician workshop (removing the largest capital and hiring barrier), a PR agency with genuine Tier-1 editorial *relationships* (Baden Bower), and high-quality offshore production labour (BruntWork). No local rival combines a Sydney master-tech workshop with a global demand-generation capability. This is why Antipode needs **~A$950k of committed capital, not A$4.2m**.
- **The gaps are equally real.** European Galleria is a mechanical service and engine-rebuild workshop — not a paint, panel, carbon or trim atelier — and there is no engineering signatory, no named design authority and no ATO ruling on Luxury Car Tax yet. Every one of these must be closed **before the first car is sold** (§4.3, §7.2).
- **The plan is sequenced proof-first.** A development mule ("build zero") is built and validated before any press, any price premium and any ramp. Three or more signed letters of intent (LOIs) gate the A$975k price; otherwise the first three cars are a founders series at A$895k.
- **Honest economics.** Year 1 (first 12 months of trading) carries **zero client deliveries** and is deliberately EBITDA-negative (≈ –A$480k) while the mule is built. The business turns EBITDA-positive in Year 2, its first delivery year, because fixed costs are inherited rather than created — the plan's central financial insight, restated honestly. Gross margin ramps from ~24% on the mule to ~36% by car #4; Year 5 EBITDA ≈ A$1.7m on six deliveries.
- **Funding.** Founder-funded: **A$950k committed in two tranches** (A$450k at JV execution; A$500k only after build zero is delivered and certified and at least one paid commission is banked). No debt; donors never on the balance sheet; peak funding need ~A$800k–1.1m. No external raise is contemplated — this is a founder-alignment plan (§10.3).

**Key findings**

1. **The donor thesis is validated by the market.** G-body 3.2 Carreras (~74,000 built) and 911 SCs (~60,000 built) are the most plentiful and affordable air-cooled 911 chassis in Australia. Even the local incumbent, Wiedergeboren, concedes "964s are hard to find; there's a lot more chassis in the 3.2s." Local G-body donors average near A$114,000 versus roughly A$175,875 for a 964. The G-body's galvanised steel unibody is the single most important durability advantage for a car meant to be driven hard in a hot, coarse-road climate.
2. **The buyer pool is small but sufficient, and export widens it.** LGT Wealth Management's 2025 State of Wealth report (with Investment Trends; 1,191 HNW Australians surveyed including 168 UHNW) found the high-net-worth population grew 10% to 760,000 in the year to June 2025, with investable assets up 18% to A$4 trillion; within that, 34,500 Australians are ultra-high-net-worth (>A$10m), a cohort that grew 19%. A domestic pool in the low tens of cars per year is defensible before any export, and the US 25-year exemption opens the world's deepest restomod market.
3. **The partnership's assets are complementary and genuinely scarce** — a factory-trained (Ferrari/Maserati) master-tech workshop operating since 2012 on one side; demand-generation, offshore production leverage and storytelling assets on the other. Together they solve the hardest problem in seven-figure car sales: credible reach to buyers.
4. **Regulation and IP are manageable but must be handled deliberately** — retain and repair the original shell and VIN (never replace the tub); VSCCS certifier on retainer from day one; ATO private binding ruling on LCT before the first sale; "reimagined by Antipode" framing with disclaimers everywhere, per the Singer lesson (§8.4).

## 3. Market & Competitive Landscape

### 3.1 Market size and demand

The global restomod and classic-restoration services market was valued at **US$7.4 billion in 2025**, per Future Market Insights' analysis, which projects the market to reach **US$7.9 billion in 2026** and to expand to **US$14.4 billion by 2036, a 6.2% CAGR**. The relevant demand figure, though, is not the global one; it is the count of Australians who can spend a million dollars on a discretionary object, and that count is rising fast (§2, finding 2). Export to the United States — where Singer, Gunther Werks and Theon have all proven seven-figure demand — is the pressure valve on a thin domestic pool.

### 5.2 Recommendation: Antipode

Adopt **Antipode**, traded as **Antipode Motor Co.**, with the descriptor line *"Porsche 911 reimagined in Australia."* One word carries the entire thesis: the antipode is the precise geographic opposite of Germany — a Stuttgart icon, re-engineered at the far end of the earth, for the far end of the earth. The one caveat is a luxury-superyacht overlap (vessels named "Antipodean" and "Antipode II"), in a different class, to be cleared with a class 12/37 search and, if needed, a coexistence view.

If Antipode fails class 12/37 clearance, adopt **Verrada** as the pre-agreed fallback rather than reopening the exercise.

### 5.3 Positioning: capability without assertion

The v4 brand voice is **quiet luxury**. The earlier external language of "Rugged Purity" and the "anti-garage-queen" stance is retired from all client-facing copy and survives only as an **internal design brief** (the engineering must still survive the Nullarbor test; we simply no longer shout about it). Externally, Australian engineering is expressed as a **provenance and validation standard**: every car is validated for heat, dust, coarse-chip roads and distance — and the validation results are published (§7.6), so the evidence speaks and the brand does not have to. The air-cooled 911 was built for autobahns and alpine passes; Australia is neither. Antipode takes the most plentiful, honest and galvanised of the classic 911s and rebuilds it for 42-degree heat, coarse-chip roads and thousand-kilometre weekends — made to be driven.

**Tone of voice:** understated, technical, confident; short sentences; numbers over adjectives; closer to a watchmaker's dossier than a car ad. Aligned to the v4 website: near-black charcoal ground, bone/parchment text, a single ochre-red accent, silent full-bleed photography, editorial pacing.

**Tagline:** **"Engineered for the other side of the world."**

### 5.4 Three pillars

1. **Engineering for this continent.** Cooling, fuelling, sealing, paint and trim chosen for Australian heat, dust and road coarseness, with a written UV/heat materials-ageing protocol. Every decision must survive the internal Nullarbor test: would this component endure a 1,200 km day on coarse chip at 42°C?
2. **The over-the-shoulder local build.** One Sydney workshop, named technicians the owner can meet, an open invitation to stand in the workshop. Selling the process alongside the product.
3. **Compliance as a feature.** Full NSW VSCCS certification as a benefit — a car you can register, insure and resell without fear. Paperwork as provenance; the certification file lives in the registry as part of the car's value.

Two supporting convictions: **analog by conviction** (no touchscreens, no drive modes; hydraulic steering feel, cable throttle) and **one of one, forever** (no two commissions share a specification; every car documented in a hardbound Build Book and entered in the Antipode registry).

### 5.5 Provenance as a product — the registry

Antipode does not sell tiers or trims. It sells **numbered, named one-off commissions** entered in a registry whose constitution is itself a product feature:

- **Registry constitution.** Every car carries a commission number and an individual name chosen with the owner. No specification is ever repeated. The registry is JV-owned transferable IP, available to owners, insurers and (with consent) future buyers.
- **Annual agreed-value revaluation.** Every car is re-valued annually under the ownership programme (§9.3), creating a documented value history from delivery.
- **Certification at resale.** Antipode inspects and re-certifies any registry car presented for resale, and maintains an **auction-house relationship** to steward the first resales into the public market.
- **Spares bank and archived materials.** Service components are banked per generation; trim hides, carpet and material off-cuts are archived per car so any future repair is invisible.
- **The Build Book as provenance artefact.** VSCCS file, dyno sheets, dimensional survey, torque records, paint readings and the photographic build journal, bound and delivered with the car — quality record, compliance evidence and resale asset in one.
- **Donor-component integrity.** Original numbered components replaced during the build are crated and returned to the owner (§7.5), preserving the option of future reversion and protecting matching-numbers value.

### 5.6 Brand architecture rules (for future programmes)

1. **Antipode** is the only master brand; the registry number and commission name identify cars ("Antipode №3 — 'Gibber'"), never trim tiers.
2. Sub-programmes (e.g. a future second hero model, certified servicing for other ateliers' cars) are endorsed descriptors — "by Antipode" — never independent brands.
3. The non-affiliation disclaimer appears on every page, document and asset, without exception.
4. No pricing is ever published; price is a private conversation per commission.
5. No third-party logos, awards badges or paid-placement marks appear in brand materials.
6. Any future market-specific programme (e.g. GCC thermal specification) is an engineering note in the registry, not a marketing badge.

## 6. Product Definition

### 6.1 Donor window and engineering menu

Focus the donor on **1979 to 1985**: the classic-silhouette, pre-Getrag 915-gearbox sweet spot with the widest supply. The build resolves the G-body's known weaknesses and turns them into story:

- Pre-1984 non-pressure-fed timing-chain tensioner → replaced with the factory's own pressure-fed Carrera-style tensioner and oil feed.
- CIS airbox backfire risk → pop-off valve.
- Rubber-centred clutch → sprung replacement.
- Dilavar head-stud fatigue on high-mileage engines → replaced during rebuild.
- Notchy 915 synchros → rebuilt gearbox.
- Ageing oil lines → full replacement.

### 6.2 Specification philosophy

Period-correct silhouette; subtle wide body (arches flared ~25–40mm, not RWB-extreme); a rebuilt and upgraded air-cooled flat-six with drivable output in the **210–260kW** range prioritising mid-range torque; five-speed manual; uprated brakes and suspension tuned for Australian B-roads; 17–18-inch Fuchs-style forged wheels with satin centres and polished lips; a cabin of leather and exposed metal with concealed modern audio; ducktail or subtle Carrera-style rear treatment; single tasteful exhaust. Palette drawn from the Australian environment: Gibber Ochre, Nullarbor Bone, Wattle, Basalt, Coastal Storm.

The signature is restraint, not aggression. **Do not chase a billet 4.4L arms race with Z/B; win on taste, provenance, drivability and story.**

## 7. Operations

### 7.1 The Artarmon ring-fenced-bays model

The build runs out of European Galleria's Artarmon workshop as the mechanical, assembly and engine-build hub. Flow: donor acquisition and inspection → full teardown and bare-metal strip → body and paint (contracted specialist under Antipode QC, with the independent inspector) → mechanical and engine rebuild with the upgrade menu (in-house, the workshop's core strength) → trim and interior (specialist sub-contract) → final assembly, calibration and road development (in-house) → VSCCS certification and registration → delivery or export.

**Capacity discipline is the operational crux.** The existing service business is the base-load revenue and must not be starved by restomod builds; equally, the builds must not be crowded out by servicing. The operating rules:

- Ring-fence build bays, technician hours and a separate scheduling board for Antipode work.
- Two separate P&Ls sharing a roof, with a market-rate services agreement (§11) built on a **take-or-pay monthly capacity block**.
- A deliberately slow ramp so the service business, cash flow and quality systems are never stressed while the process is proven.
- European Galleria is paid a market labour rate (~A$120/hr blended, ASSUMPTION — **benchmarked in writing against the Sydney prestige-marque range of A$150–220/hr**, §11) for every Antipode build hour. Luigi is never diluted twice.

**Capacity arithmetic (Y5 check).** Six cars at ~2,700 total hours each ≈ 16,200 build hours; at ~1,700 productive hours per technician-year that is **9–10 technicians**, against a current workshop team that must also carry the service base-load. The hiring plan (§7.2, stages 2–4) adds one senior technician in Y1, a panel/paint lead at the Y2 gate, a trim specialist and first apprentice in Y3, and two further technicians across Y4–Y5; the Artarmon footprint supports this with the booth option fitted, but is re-checked at the Y3 planning gate (§13).

### 7.2 Build zero (the mule) — prototype economics, disclosed

The first car is explicitly a **development mule**: prototype economics are expected and disclosed. It is sold to a known, sympathetic client at a disclosed discount against the standard commission (inside the founders-series pricing), on the explicit understanding that it proves the process. The mule's purpose is to generate three assets no amount of money can shortcut: (1) a validated physical car; (2) a measured hours-and-cost baseline that replaces this plan's assumptions with actuals; (3) the published validation record that anchors all later PR (§9.1). No press, no price premium and no ramp decision happens before the mule is painted, running and validated.

BruntWork's role is deliberately re-scoped from v3: **CAD support (reporting to the design authority), CRM administration, auction and donor-market monitoring, and asset production only.** It has **no client contact and no role in Journal or build-record content** — anything a client sees, touches or reads comes from the Sydney team, and Jeremy owns all client contact personally until Year 3. ### 7.5 Donor acquisition, procurement concierge & donor-engine policy

**The balance-sheet principle (hard rule): donor vehicles never sit on the atelier's balance sheet.**

1. **Client-supplied donor (target ~60% of builds).** Antipode approves the client's car against a **140-point donor suitability checklist** (chassis-number provenance, rust mapping, accident history, matching numbers where relevant, galvanising integrity, prior repair quality). The **VSCCS certifier countersigns the donor pre-purchase inspection** so certification risk is surfaced before money moves; per-inspection fees (~A$500–800 × 3–4 inspections per purchase) are budgeted in the concierge P&L.
2. **Concierge-procured donor (~40%).** Antipode sources the car from the Australian, US or Japanese market **against client funds held in escrow**, title passing directly to the client. The atelier acts as procurement agent, never principal.

**Procurement process:** client brief → market sweep (dealer networks, PCA classifieds, Shannons/Collecting Cars, US/Japanese agents) → shortlist with inspection reports → independent mechanical inspection → negotiation and purchase into escrow → transport and quarantine → handover into teardown.

**Concierge P&L per procured donor (indicative, ASSUMPTIONS):**

| Line | A$ |
|---|---|
| Concierge fee charged to client (7.5% of donor price, min. A$9k) | 11,250 (on A$150k donor) |
| Search, inspection & agent costs (incl. 3–4 paid inspections) | (4,500) |
| Transport, quarantine, compliance (import cases, avg.) | (4,200) |

| **Contribution per procured donor** | **~1,750** |

The fee is deliberately modest: the concierge exists to de-risk the pipeline and control donor quality, not to maximise margin. Its true value is strategic — the atelier never stalls a A$1.1m commission for want of a A$120k car, and it builds proprietary G-body pricing intelligence that compounds over time.

**Donor-engine policy.** The original engine is retained and rebuilt wherever the block is sound; replaced numbered components are crated, documented and returned to the owner, preserving matching-numbers provenance.

**Donor supply security:** standing non-exclusive MOUs with three Australian prestige dealers, two US west-coast 911 specialists and one Japanese exporter; a continuously refreshed watchlist of 25–40 suitable cars (BruntWork-assisted); donor secured or client car approved no later than Month 1 of any signed commission; and a **quality floor** — cars failing the rust-map threshold are rejected regardless of price, because rectification beyond threshold destroys build economics.

### 7.6 Build stages, quality gates and the validation protocol

Total build content per Foundation car is budgeted at **~3,000 hours** (ASSUMPTION), split honestly between **~2,180 in-house billed hours** (teardown/foundation ~620; fabrication & engineering ~900; assembly & systems ~380; validation & certification ~280) and **~820 subcontracted hours** (paint & body ~340; trim ~480) that are paid as subcontract lines, not labour (§10.1). **The written 1,500 km validation protocol** (results published in the Journal for every car): (1) 42°C heat-soak and hot-restart testing with full thermal logging; (2) dust-seal test on unsealed roads; (3) coarse-chip ride route with noise/harshness measurement; (4) a 1,200 km endurance day; (5) dyno certification and oil-consumption baseline; (6) UV/heat materials-ageing checks per the materials protocol. This protocol is the brand's evidence — it is what lets the voice stay quiet.

**The build-standards book.** Craftsmanship is codified without diluting hand-built character: panel-gap tolerance **3.5mm ± 0.5**, paint film thickness ranges per panel, torque-marking discipline on every fastener, loom routing and clipping standards, and fastener/plating finish schedules. Every build phase closes with a signed inspection gate; every torque value, dyno sheet and paint reading is logged into the car's digital build record, which becomes the **Build Book**. Process documentation also de-risks key-person dependency.

## 8. Regulatory, Tax & IP

### 8.1 VSCCS certification — the moat, not the overhead

Significantly modified vehicles in NSW must be certified under the **Vehicle Safety Compliance Certification Scheme (VSCCS)** by a Transport for NSW-licensed certifier, against VSB14/NCOP and the modification codes in Vehicle Standards Information sheet 6. Engine swaps, brake and suspension upgrades, body modifications and seat/belt changes all fall within licensed modification codes, and certifiers are only authorised within their licensed sub-categories. Without certification the car cannot legally hold registration — which destroys usability, insurability and resale value. **Antipode treats certification as a designed-in feature from CAD stage, never a retrofit.**

**Certifier strategy:** engage a certifier whose licence covers the full set of needed modification codes, **on retainer from day one**, involved from donor pre-purchase (§7.5) through build one so staged inspection photographs and evidence are captured as work proceeds.

| Step | Timing | Action | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Pre-build engineering brief | Design freeze | Certifier reviews spec against applicable ADR schedule (braking ADR 31/35, lighting ADR 13, glazing ADR 8, seatbelt anchorages ADR 5, steering ADR 10, emissions/noise per category and build date) | Certifier + Luigi |
| 2. Design compliance matrix | Engineering phase | Clause-by-clause compliance matrix embedded in the build record; deviations resolved before metal is cut | Certifier + build lead |
| 3. In-build evidence capture | Months 4–13 | Photographic and measurement evidence per modification (weld maps, anchorage pull-test data, brake line specs) | Build team |
| 4. Inspection & testing | Validation phase | Physical inspection; brake performance test; noise/emissions-equivalence testing | Certifier |
| 5. Certificate issue | Pre-delivery | VSCCS certificate issued; modification plate fitted; file archived in registry as provenance | Certifier |
| 6. Registration support | Delivery | Certificate presented to state authority; first registration executed with client | Concierge |

### 8.2 ICV risk — the absolute rule

If the original tub is replaced rather than repaired, the car risks **Individually Constructed Vehicle (ICV)** treatment — a heavier and more expensive path. The operational rule is absolute: **retain, repair and seam-strengthen the original galvanised G-body shell and its VIN; never replace the tub.** This also protects identity, resale integrity and the "restored, not built-from-scratch" story.

### 8.3 Luxury Car Tax — do not guess

LCT generally does not apply to vehicles more than 25 years old, but there is genuine risk that a newly re-manufactured vehicle is treated differently. **Obtain an ATO private binding ruling on the LCT treatment of a G-body restomod before the first sale**, and price with a contingency until the ruling is in hand. If the ruling brings LCT into scope, **re-price toward ~A$1.3m** to preserve the gross-margin floor and disclose to the waitlist before converting deposits — with the demand implication honestly modelled (§10.6). (The current ATO position could not be independently re-verified for this plan; flagged as an open item, §16.)

### 8.4 Trademark strategy and the Singer lesson (retained)

Never market or invoice "a Porsche." Always *"a Porsche 911 reimagined by Antipode,"* with the non-affiliation disclaimer on every page, document and social asset: *"Antipode Motor Co. is an independent restorer and is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or connected to Dr. Ing. h.c. F. Porsche AG. 'Porsche,' '911' and 'Carrera' are trademarks of Porsche AG, used here for descriptive purposes only."*

The Singer matter is instructive: Porsche Cars North America filed suit against Singer Vehicle Design (No. 1:24-cv-00253, D. Del., filed 26 February 2024) over 911 trade dress; Porsche withdrew on 22 March 2024 after Singer amended its website wording (adding that builds are done "at the direction of its clients"). The lesson is precise: keep the Antipode identity dominant, avoid Porsche crests and script, avoid copying protected design cues wholesale, frame everything as client-directed restoration, and clear the brand name in class 12 and 37 (plus a US knock-out search) before any public launch.

## 9. Go-To-Market

### 9.1 Earned media only — what changed and why

v3 proposed a guaranteed-placement PR wave and a commissioned award. Both are deleted: UHNW buyers and their advisors can identify paid placement instantly, and a guaranteed-placement footprint or a founder-owned pay-to-play award attached to a seven-figure atelier is **existentially brand-damaging**. Accordingly:

- **No Global Recognition Awards involvement of any kind** — not in GTM, not in credibility-building, not anywhere in this plan.
- **No guaranteed placements.** Baden Bower's role is redefined to what is genuinely valuable: editorial *relationships*, story craft and timing. Target outlets are earned-editorial only — Octane, Evo, Classic & Sports Car, AFR Luxury — approached at **genuine news moments**: the named design authority announcement, publication of the engineering menu, and above all the first validated car with its published test results.
- **No press until proof.** No media outreach until build zero is painted, running and has completed the 1,500 km validation protocol (§7.6).
- **Paid-content labelling discipline.** Any paid placement is labelled as advertising, kept out of motoring editorial contexts, and never presented as coverage.

### 9.2 Four phases, re-sequenced

**Phase 1 — Quiet preparation (months 0–6).** Secure the name, disclaimers and domains. Build the homepage and a restrained render suite (art direction per §5.3). Announce the **named design authority** and publish the **engineering menu** — two legitimate news items that require no car to exist. Open the LOI process (§3.3). Start the mule.

**Phase 2 — Proof (months 6–18).** Build and validate the mule. Publish the validation results in the Journal. **Phase 3 — Collector-network activation (ongoing).** Direct presence where the buyers already are: Porsche Club NSW events, concours and track days; curated presence on Collecting Cars and Shannons auctions. **Phase 4 — Export (Year 3 onward).** Use the US 25-year exemption and Baden Bower's US editorial relationships to open American commissions once domestic proof points exist; retain US and GCC collector advisors on 4–6% success fees for qualified introductions; one international showcase (Monterey Car Week or Goodwood) in Year 3.

### 9.3 The ownership programme, years 1–10

## 11. JV & Governance — term-sheet-level detail

The JV and its services agreements are load-bearing contracts, not formalities. Recommended terms:

1. **Entity.** A new clean company — **"Antipode Motor Co. Pty Ltd"** — ring-fencing liability from both founders' existing businesses. The JV **owns**: the brand and trademarks, the jigs and body bucks, the registry, the Build Book archive, the design-language book and all build IP.
2. **Equity.** 50/50 between the founders' holding entities, reflecting broadly equal (and hard-to-replace) contributions. Founder vesting over four years with standard good/bad-leaver provisions, given the acute key-person dependence on Luigi.
3. **Deadlock.** Operational deadlocks → **expert determination** by a pre-agreed independent marque expert. Strategic deadlocks → **mediation**, then a **buy-sell mechanism with independent valuation** (each side may offer to buy at the independent valuer's figure; a naked shotgun clause is explicitly rejected as it favours the deeper pocket).
4. **Reserved matters** (require both founders' consent): any price change to the commission basis; any new build programme or model line; capex above A$150k outside the approved plan; any related-party contract or variation; taking on debt; admission of any investor; brand licensing; any public statement deviating from the §8.4 trademark discipline.
5. **European Galleria services agreement (the load-bearing one).** (a) **Take-or-pay monthly capacity block**: the JV commits to a fixed monthly block of technician hours and bay capacity at ~A$120/hr blended — a rate **benchmarked in writing against the Sydney prestige-marque market range of A$150–220/hr**, reviewed annually. (b) **≥18-month termination notice** from either side, so no client car is ever stranded mid-build. (c) **Step-in and WIP protection**: client cars are held under escrow documentation; on any termination the JV has completion rights (access to bays, jigs and records) and secure storage rights for client property. (d) The JV owns all jigs and build IP created with its funds, even if fabricated in the Artarmon workshop.
6. **Founder-contributed services.** Baden Bower (PR relationships and story craft) and BruntWork (behind-the-wall production) provide services at cost or as documented in-kind contributions, credited to Jeremy's capital account at independently reviewed valuations. **Global Recognition Awards has no role.**
7. **Related-party discipline.** All related-party contracts (services agreement, contributed services, premises) require **both founders' written consent and an annual external review** by the JV's accountant, with a related-party schedule in the management accounts.
8. **Key-person and restraint.** Key-person insurance of **~A$2m on Luigi with the JV as beneficiary**; a restraint and IP-assignment deed from Luigi covering build methods, client relationships and the registry; Jeremy's corresponding deed covering brand, media and client-relationship IP.
9. **Founder remuneration policy.** No founder drawings during Years 1–2; founders are remunerated through the documented services agreements; dividends only from Year 3 positive NPAT, by board resolution.

A business that can prove 12+ months of forward-sold A$1m+ commissions, a certification moat, a cash-positive service base-load and zero balance-sheet donor risk is a fundamentally more acquirable asset than one that cannot.
